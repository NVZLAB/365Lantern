# Suspicious Behavior

Findings starts with a separate Suspicious Behavior block, ordered by priority, ahead of the full account timeline. Selecting an indicator displays its explanation and all referenced original evidence. The block and suspicious.json / suspicious.csv are generated by the same versioned detector. The ZIP manifest hashes both new files. Empty results produce an empty JSON list and a CSV header, never a clean-account verdict.

## Rules (version 1)

- Existing sign-in and current forwarding/rule detectors are included. Their original explanations and evidence references remain available.
- Potential impossible travel: consecutive successful sign-ins for the same account, different canonical IPs, usable Microsoft coordinates, at least 500 km, more than 900 km/h, within 24 hours. Missing/placeholder coordinates, zero elapsed time, failed sign-ins and different accounts do not satisfy this rule. Coordinates describe approximate network locations, not a person's physical location; VPNs/proxies and shared sessions can explain the pattern. No external IP geolocation service is used.
- Historical inbox-rule changes: New-InboxRule, Set-InboxRule and UpdateInboxRules. Explicit forwarding/redirection or visibility-changing parameters increase priority. Other rule changes are medium-priority review items, not automatic compromise conclusions. Set-Mailbox forwarding changes are included. Explicit failed audit results are excluded; unreported outcomes remain unconfirmed in the explanation.
- Permission changes: mailbox permission/delegation commands, plus successful directory role grants, app-role assignments, delegated-permission grants and application consent. Original actor, targets, AccessRights and Deny values require review. Routine authorized administration can trigger these indicators.
- Microsoft mail threats: documented ThreatIntelligence records with phishing/malware in Verdict or structured ThreatsAndDetectionTech. These are recorded verdicts and may have been revised later.
- Suspect mail flow/access: match the same account and exact Internet Message-ID between a recorded threat and trace or MailItemsAccessed/MessageBind evidence. MailItemsAccessed supports nested Folders/FolderItems message IDs. Access may be automated or aggregated; it does not prove a human opened/read/clicked an email. Transport evidence does not prove the account owner personally sent a message. Message-ID matches are evidence links, not proof of causation or cryptographic identity.

No rule guesses phishing from a subject line or labels every delivered message suspicious. No message body or attachment is fetched. Safe Links click telemetry remains uncollected.

## Best available evidence

In addition to existing audit queries, attempt a separate Search-UnifiedAuditLog ThreatIntelligence text search for each current mailbox address (maximum 20). Retain exact P1/P2 sender or recipient address matches. Deduplicate audit IDs across queries and cap retained unified audit evidence at 50,000 unique records per account. Reaching the cap preserves existing evidence and reports partial/unavailable remaining coverage. This search is best effort: unavailable commands, read permissions, licensing, retention, aliases, text-index behavior and result limits can prevent evidence collection. Each query gets its own coverage result, and failure does not block independent sources. No additional permissions are automatically granted or requested by this change.

The suspicious files contain indicators and references, not the entire timeline. Raw records and coverage remain in evidence.json. JSON includes investigation scope, synthetic/import provenance, rule version and interpretation limits; CSV includes scope, synthetic status, rule version, severity, basis, timestamp, account, explanation and evidence references. CSV fields are escaped and formula prefixes neutralized.

## Validation and references

Synthetic checks exercise geography false positives, account boundaries, failed changes, nested mail-access IDs, exact-message correlations, missing/malformed verdicts, CSV injection protection and archive integrity. Live service validation is still required for actual threat-audit availability and schemas.
